THE MARCH TO IMPACT
WHICH MARCH MADNESS TEAMS ACTUALLY MOVE THE WAGE NEEDLE?
Forget seeds — these are the 68 tournament teams ranked by how much they actually raise graduates' earnings above what a high school diploma would have gotten them, adjusted for regional cost of living, graduation rates, and school accessibility. Spoiler: of 68 teams leave students worse off than if they'd never enrolled.
IMPACT — The headline score. Combines earning power, graduation rate, acceptance rate, and school size into one number. Higher = this school lifts more people's wages by more dollars. A school that's accessible to many and still delivers strong returns beats an elite school that cherry-picks 500 students.
ADJ ROI — Adjusted Return on Investment ($K over a career). The lifetime financial gain of attending this school vs. not attending college at all, after factoring in graduation rates and tuition costs. Dropouts get zero benefit but still paid tuition, so low-grad-rate schools get hammered here.
COL GAIN — Cost-of-Living-Adjusted Annual Gain ($/yr vs. HS). How much more a graduate earns per year than a high-school-only worker in the same region, converted to real purchasing power. A $55K salary in Iowa buys more than $65K in New York — this column reflects that.
GRAD% — Six-year graduation rate. The % of students who actually finish and get the degree. Schools under 50% are sending most students home with debt and no credential.
ACC% — Acceptance rate. Higher = more accessible. A 90%-acceptance school delivering strong ROI is doing something remarkable. A 5% school is filtering for talent that was going to succeed regardless.
SIZE — Total enrollment in thousands. Larger schools affect more lives.
RPP — Regional Price Parity (BEA 2024). The cost-of-living index for the school's state. 100 = national average. Below 100 = cheaper (salary stretches further). Above 100 = more expensive.
DRAW — Student draw type. N = National (≤25% accept → compared to national HS baseline). B = Broad (26–60% → blended baseline). R = Regional (>60% → compared to state HS baseline).
COST — Average annual net price after financial aid ($K/yr). What students actually pay out of pocket.
T — Tier grade: S=Elite A=Strong B=Solid C=Break Even D=Negative F=Deep Negative

The Model — Step by Step:

1. State HS Baseline: The "would I have been better off skipping college?" bar. Nationally, a full-time worker with only a high school diploma earns a median of $50K/yr (BLS Q1 2025: $953/wk × 52). But this varies by state: we scale it by each state's Regional Price Parity (BEA 2024). Iowa (RPP 87.8) → local HS baseline of $43.9K. New York (RPP 106.2) → $53.1K. California (RPP 110.7) → $55.4K.

2. Draw Classification: Not every school should be judged against the national average. A regional state school in Alabama mostly serves Alabama residents who'll work in Alabama — so we compare them to Alabama's HS baseline, not Manhattan's. We classify by acceptance rate: National (≤25%, elite national draw → national baseline), Broad (26–60% → 50/50 blend), Regional (>60% → state baseline).

3. COL-Adjusted Earnings: A graduate's median earnings 10 years after enrollment (from College Scorecard) are converted to real purchasing power: Real Earnings = Nominal × (100 / RPP). A $58K salary in Iowa is worth $66K in national purchasing power, while $70K in California is only worth $63K.

4. True Annual Gain vs. HS: = COL-Adjusted Earnings − Blended HS Baseline. This is the real annual wage premium. It can be negative — meaning graduates literally earn less than local HS-only workers, even after adjusting for cost of living.

5. Adjusted ROI (lifetime): = GradRate × (Annual Gain × 40yrs − Tuition × 4.5yrs) + (1 − GradRate) × (0 − Tuition × 2yrs)
Graduates get the 40-year earnings premium minus full tuition. Dropouts get zero premium but still paid ~2 years of tuition before leaving. This is why schools with 30–40% grad rates can have deeply negative ROI.

6. Impact Score: = Adj ROI × (Accept Rate / 50) × log₂(Enrollment / 5K)
The final ranking metric. Multiplies the school's ROI by how many people can actually access it. A massive state school with 80% acceptance and decent ROI beats a tiny elite school with amazing ROI but only 5% acceptance. The log scale on enrollment prevents mega-universities from dominating on size alone.
